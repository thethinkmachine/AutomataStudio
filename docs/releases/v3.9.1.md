## What's Changed

### Faster
* **Testing words is several times faster.** The Test Words table, the Language panel, StateMate's checks and `automata test` all decide words through the same engine, and it now reads a machine as a table of numbers instead of looking transitions up by name. Measured on one laptop: a DFA 17 ns → 6 ns a symbol, a Moore machine 71 → 11, a Mealy machine 78 → 24.
* **NFAs and ε-NFAs: up to 80× on long words.** A run remembers the sets of states it has already been in, so a set met again costs one lookup. A 64-state NFA went from 1.6 µs to 19 ns a symbol; a 1,024-state one from 39 µs to 3.4 µs.
* **Turing machines and LBAs: 4–8× when deciding.** The tape is an array of codes and the loop check is two integers kept up to date as the head moves. A sweeping machine went from 190 ns to 25 ns a step, the five-state busy beaver from 88 to 18, and the bundled binary adder and LBA examples from about 4 ms to about 1.2 ms a word. Verdicts are unchanged, including when a loop is found.
* **Two-way automata and transducers: 4–8×.** 2DFA 190 → 40 ns a symbol, 2DFT 460 → 54.
* **Long input is read faster.** Over an alphabet of one-character symbols, a typed word is split 3–4× faster.
* **ω-automata** decide 20–40% faster.
* **Playback spends less time drawing what you cannot see.** While a run is playing or running to its end, the trace log and the step tracker are not redrawn if their section is folded or their tab is behind another. Open one mid-run and it fills on the next frame; when the run stops, everything is drawn.

### Fixed
* **A Turing machine with multi-character tape symbols is no longer reported as looping when it is not.** Two different tapes could read as the same one when their symbols ran together (`ab`,`c` and `a`,`bc`), and the word was rejected as a loop. Tapes are now compared cell by cell.
