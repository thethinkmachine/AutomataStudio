### The dialogs

**Every `.overlay` is the same three-part shell**, and what a dialog declares about itself is separate from its markup. The markup is `.overlay` (the scrim, and its id is the handle) → `.modal` (the card) → `.modal-title` / body / `.modal-foot`; the behaviour is `registerModal(id, {onClose, submit, onEscape, dismissOnBackdrop, initialFocus})` in [js/modal.js](js/modal.js), which also owns the stack, the Tab trap, the topmost-wins Escape and Enter-to-submit. Adding a dialog is markup plus one registration — never a branch in `closeModal`.

Points worth keeping in mind:

- **A closed overlay is `visibility: hidden`, and that is not cosmetic.** All thirteen dialogs live in the DOM permanently. `opacity: 0` and `pointer-events: none` hide a thing from the eye and the pointer but leave every control inside it in the page's focus order, so Tab from the toolbar used to walk into more than a hundred invisible controls — 56 of them in Settings. It is *transitioned* on the way out, because `visibility` interpolates discretely with one special case: when either endpoint is `visible`, visible holds for every point strictly *between* them, so the dialog stays painted through its own fade-out. **On the way in it switches instantly** (`visibility 0s` on `.show`), and that is not cosmetic either: at t = 0 of a hidden → visible transition the value is still `hidden`, `showOverlay` calls `focus()` on the same line that adds the class, and `focus()` on a hidden element is a silent no-op. With both directions transitioned, every dialog opened with focus left on the page behind the scrim and no `initialFocus` ever landed — invisible to the suite, since the stub computes no styles, which is why [tests/modal.test.js](../../../tests/modal.test.js) reads the rule. The `offsetParent !== null` filter in `modalFocusables` only means what it looks like it means because of the base rule — inside a closed `position: fixed` overlay, every child has an offsetParent.
- **The title is an `<h2>` and the id is on a `<span>` inside it.** That split is what lets the close button be injected: several dialogs rewrite their title with `textContent` (the transition editor, the divider editor, the update dialog, every confirm) and the wizard rebuilds its title on every step, so a button written into the element being rewritten would not survive. Writing to the span cannot reach a sibling. `aria-labelledby` points at that span everywhere — five dialogs used to repeat their title in `aria-label` instead, which is two strings that can drift.
- **`installModalChrome()` gives every dialog its close button**, on each open and idempotently, rather than the button being written thirteen times. Not one dialog had one; About and Keyboard Shortcuts have a single focusable control between them, and a touch device has no Escape. A dialog with no `.modal-title` — About leads with its logo lockup — gets the absolutely positioned `.modal-close-float` variant instead.
- **The title and footer are sticky, and `--modal-px` / `--modal-py` are why.** In a dialog taller than the viewport the heading used to scroll off the top and the actions off the bottom. Both bars bleed to the dialog's edges with a negative margin matching its padding, so the padding is a pair of custom properties rather than a literal — `css/mobile.css` retunes the tokens and the margins follow. The three wide dialogs (`.export-code-modal`, `.settings-modal-wide`, `.wizard-modal-wide`) are already flex columns with `overflow: hidden`, where a sticky child is inert; the wizard's title and footer override the bleed margins explicitly, as a shorthand, so tuning one side cannot leave the other three inherited.
- **The sticky inset is `calc(var(--modal-py) * -1)`, not `0`, and that is what makes the bleed above actually bleed.** A sticky inset is resolved against the element's *margin* box, so the bar's negative margin is subtracted from the offset the browser pins to: with `top: 0` the title came to rest `--modal-py` *below* the scrollport and the footer the same distance above it, opening a strip at each end of every scrolling dialog that the body passed through in the open and was cut off in — a row of fields sliced through the middle with nothing over it, which reads as a rendering fault rather than as a scroll. Cancelling the margin in the inset pins each bar's border box flush to the edge it belongs to. It only ever mattered for the sticky bars: `.about-modal-wide`'s footer is `position: static`, where the negative margin bleeds on its own.
- **The scroll shadows are `--tab-overflow-shadow`.** That is the app's existing "there is more past this edge" colour, already carried by the workspace tab strip and the algorithm rail, so the affordance reads the same wherever it appears and follows the theme — a literal `rgba(0, 0, 0, …)` is invisible against a dark ground, which is half the palette range these dialogs are read in. `js/modal.js` sets `is-scrolled` and `has-more`; the base declares the shadow as `transparent` so it fades rather than snapping on at the first pixel of scroll.
- **`role="alertdialog"` on the two that ask a destructive question** (confirm, unsaved), each with `aria-describedby` on the sentence that asks it. `.confirm-msg` is `--sans`: it is a sentence addressed to the reader, and it was the one place in the app where plain English was set in the identifier face.
- **`initialFocus` is a registration, not a second `focus()` call.** The fallback is "first focusable in DOM order, skipping the injected close button" — the × sits in the title bar, ahead of everything the dialog is for, and is taken only when it is the one control there is. That is still a Cancel as often as it is the field the reader came to fill in. The note and divider editors used to correct that with a `setTimeout` racing the open animation — a second, visible focus move.

**The tabbed dialogs are one strip twice.** Engine Settings and Keyboard Shortcuts share a shell and a switcher (`switchModalTab` in [js/ui.js](js/ui.js)); their tabs are `<button role="tab">`s with `aria-selected`, a roving tabindex and Arrow/Home/End, which is the treatment [js/panel-state.js](js/panel-state.js) gives the sidebars and the one strip in the app that had not had it. They were `<div onclick>`s, so seven settings panels and five shortcut panels could not be reached from a keyboard at all.

Two things there are easy to get wrong. **`hidden` is what hides a panel** — the same rule `css/panels.css` states for the sidebar tabpanels, and the reason `.modal-tab-content.active { display: block }` had to go: at (0,2,0) it outranks `[hidden]` at (0,1,1), so setting the attribute would have done nothing while the class was on. `.active` survives as the state class the strip styles from, and `sizeSettingsPanels()` toggles the *attribute* to measure, or it measures a stack of `display: none` boxes. And **the target is resolved by id, the deselection by `querySelectorAll`** — not one sweep doing both, because the target has to be reachable when `querySelectorAll` returns nothing, which is how the tests observe this.

**A shortcut label is a spec, written as text in one place.** [js/kbd.js](../../../js/kbd.js) is import-free: `formatKbd('Mod+Shift+T')` is `⌘⇧T` on Apple platforms and `Ctrl+Shift+T` elsewhere, and `Ctrl` in a spec reads as `Mod` because every key handler reads `ctrlKey || metaKey`. Markup carries `data-kbd="Mod+Z"` with the non-Apple spelling as its own text, rewritten at boot by `localizeKbdLabels()`; `data-tip-kbd` goes through the same function in the tooltip; prose that names a key ("one Ctrl+Z puts it back") interpolates it. There were three dialects before — `Ctrl+Z`, `⌘Z` on Linux, `Ctrl/Cmd+B` — and [tests/kbd.test.js](../../../tests/kbd.test.js) fails on a `⌘` written into the page.

**Notation keeps its case under an uppercase label.** `text-transform` maps letters, not words: δ became Δ (the output alphabet), ω became Ω (the priority function), ε became Ε. Wrap notation in `.sym`; `keepSymbolCase()` in `reference.js` does it for guide data.

**Dialog buttons are two bases and colour modifiers.** `.btn-p` / `.btn-g` declare the box, the type and every state once — `:hover`, `:active`, `:focus-visible`, `:disabled`, `.is-busy` — so a modifier (`.btn-danger-ghost`, `.btn-danger`) only ever changes colour. Three things that used to be missing and are worth not re-losing:

- **A disabled state exists.** There was none, so a gated primary painted exactly like a live one and no dialog could express "not yet".
- **The icon slot is the base's** (`inline-flex` + gap, with `.btn-icon` for the glyph). `.wiz-primary` existed only to add those three lines back to `.btn-p`.
- **Position is `.modal-foot-start`'s job, not a colour variant's.** `.btn-danger-ghost` carried `margin-right: auto`, which left-pinned every destructive button whether or not that was wanted and forced four `!important`s to win against `.btn-g`; written as `.btn-g.btn-danger-ghost` it wins on specificity for free.

The face is `--sans`. These are language — "Cancel", "Export", "Download" — and `--mono` is for identifiers and instrumentation, which is the rule the StateMate console already followed and the reason a button there and a button in a dialog did not read as the same control.

### The sidebars

**The two panels are one component twice**, and everything that says so is in one place each. [js/panel-state.js](js/panel-state.js) is the import-free registry of tabs, sides and selection; `syncPanelTabs` / `activatePanelTab` / `showPanelTab` / `revealPanel` / `togglePanelPin` in [js/ui.js](js/ui.js) each take a side or read one, rather than existing twice; and [css/panels.css](css/panels.css) holds both panels plus the chrome they share (`.panel-header`, `.panel-tabs`, `.panel-tab`, `.pin-btn`, `.panel-resizer`, `.mobile-panel-close`).

That file is the point. The left panel's rules lived in `css/lpanel.css`, the right panel's in `views.css` and its tab strip in `modals.css`, which is how the two drifted: different header padding, a title where the other had tabs, and — the one that mattered — `.lpanel.unpinned` hiding a *hand-picked list* of its children while `.rpanel.unpinned` hid all of them, so a third tabpanel hosted on the left would have stayed painted over the canvas with the panel collapsed to its hover rail. Both now hide `> :not(style)`.

`togglePanelPin(side)` is the same shape of fix: it was two copies differing in three string literals. `toggleLPanelPin`/`toggleRPanelPin` survive as one-line wrappers only because `bridge.js` and the `onclick=` attributes know those names.

**A tab is a seam, drawn by the tab rather than by overlapping the header's rule.** The selected tab has rounded top corners, a hairline outline, and the panel body's own `--bg2`; the strip sits flush against `.panel-header`'s `border-bottom` and the active tab's own `border-bottom: 1px solid var(--bg2)` (with `margin-bottom: -1px`) covers that rule's pixel so the tab and the panel body meet with nothing between them. The line separates chrome from content everywhere except under the tab you are reading, where the two are the same thing — which is the whole metaphor, and it is why the accent rides the tab's *top* edge: under the tab is where the seam border is, and a second rule there would be drawing back what the shape just removed.

Two things that shape depends on. **The header keeps `--bg2`** — see the note over `header` in [css/layout.css](css/layout.css): the app header and both panels are meant to read as one surface, and recessing this strip would cut it horizontally the way a hairline there would cut it vertically. So the contrast comes from the other side: **`.panel-tab:not(.active)` is recessed toward `--bg`**, which is the ground under everything and darker than `--bg2` in every theme, so it deepens whichever way a palette runs. Without that, an outline on a header and a body of identical paint is a curve drawn on flat colour. And **the border is `transparent` on every tab** rather than present only on the selected one, or the label shifts a pixel each way as selection moves.

Two more worth keeping straight. **A strip holds one tab or two** — `grid-auto-flow: column` rather than a fixed pair, because StateMate moving changes both counts at once — and **a lone tab drops the shape entirely**, outline, fill and cap, since with nothing to be selected *instead of* a tab cut out of the header rule says nothing. **`.panel-header`'s bottom padding is zero on both sides** now that both carry a strip: the strip's own 36px is what meets the header's 44px minimum, and the tab has to reach the header's bottom edge to break its rule.

**A row in States Q and a row in Transitions δ end in the same pair of controls**, built once by `rowActions()` in [js/render.js](js/render.js) and styled once as `.lp-row-btn` in [css/panels.css](css/panels.css). They had drifted into halves of one control: a state carried a pencil and no way to delete, a transition a delete and no way to edit — and the delete was a `<span onclick>` where the pencil was a `<button>`, so of the two only the pencil could be reached by Tab or fired with Enter. Both are buttons now, both reveal on `:hover` *and* `:focus-within`, with an `@media (hover: none)` rule that keeps them shown where there is no hover to reveal them with. The pair sits in a `.lp-row-acts` wrapper rather than taking `margin-left: auto` on the first button, or hiding one of the two moves the other; `.lp-row-body` holds everything the row *says*, so a long name truncates instead of pushing the buttons off the panel. Editing a transition from the list goes through `editTransFromList`, which resolves the edge and opens the same dialog `ctxEditTrans` opens from the canvas — the list and the diagram must not be two ways to edit the same row differently.

**Both lists draw a window onto their data, not a row per item.** [js/panel-list.js](js/panel-list.js) is handed the array and a row renderer; `updateLPanel` supplies the markup and `filterStates`/`filterTransitions` supply the query. A 1000-state machine is three thousand rows with two buttons and five inline handlers each, rebuilt from one innerHTML string on every structural edit — into a panel 300px tall, where twenty of them are visible. Three things it has to get right:

- **The filter moves to the data.** Hiding rows that were never built is meaningless, so the search box narrows the array and redraws. It also stopped being a second full walk setting `style.display` on every row.
- **Scroll position survives a redraw.** `updateLPanel` runs on every graph change — including the ones a row's own buttons cause — and an innerHTML write resets `scrollTop`, so deleting the fortieth transition threw the reader back to the top.
- **The pitch is measured off two drawn rows, not assumed.** Row height follows the theme's font and the panel's padding, both of which the reader changes. The spacers subtract one `gap`, because the flex gap adds one more between a spacer and the row beside it — without that the list grows by a gap per page and the scrollbar drifts away from the content it indexes.

Below `WINDOW_MIN_ROWS` the list is drawn whole, spacers and measurement included, so the small-machine path is exactly what it was.

**The docked left panel gives its spare height to one list.** Every list is capped at `--lp-list-max-h`, which left ten transitions scrolling in a 168px box above two hundred pixels of empty panel. `syncDockFill(side)` in [js/panel-sections-ui.js](../../../js/panel-sections-ui.js) marks the *last* open section that declares a `fill` region, and that region takes the leftover — the cap becomes its floor, so a panel with no room scrolls exactly as it did. The last, because growing it moves nothing but the collapsed headers under it. It is asked of the DOM on every collapse, reorder, tear-off, dock and machine switch, because an id stopped being the right key the moment sections could move (it used to be `#lp-transitions { flex: 1 }`). The region is `flex: 1 1 0`, sized to the leftover alone: a windowed list slices its rows from its own height, and a box as tall as its content would draw every row. Left panel only (`dockFill` in the registry) — the right panel's regions are drawn boxes, and a stretched trace log is a tall empty card.

**A tab name is an element, not a text node.** `text-overflow` has nothing to apply to on the anonymous flex item a loose string becomes, so `.panel-tab-label` is what ellipsizes; before it, a name simply drew over the next tab, since the tracks are `minmax(0, 1fr)` and a tab is *allowed* to be narrower than its own text. Below 300px — measured by `@container`, because these panels are resized and pinned independently of the window — the tab icon is dropped rather than the words, two glyphs that repeat the names being cheaper to lose than the ends of both names.
